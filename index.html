📅 Daily Routines & Habits

Build systems that stick without burning out

🌅 The 5-Minute Morning Reset

Most morning routines fail because they're too ambitious. Start with just 5 minutes:

  • Hydrate: Drink 500ml water immediately after waking
  • Breathe: 10 deep breaths by an open window
  • Prioritize: Write down your #1 goal for the day

💡 Success Rate: 87% stick with this for 30+ days vs 23% with hour-long routines

🎯 The 2-Minute Rule

Any habit can be started in 2 minutes or less. Want to read more? Don't commit to "30 pages daily" — commit to "opening the book." Momentum builds naturally from there.

⏰ Habit Stacking Formula

Attach new habits to existing ones: "After I [current habit], I will [new habit]"

  • After I brush my teeth, I'll do 10 pushups
  • After I pour my coffee, I'll review my calendar
  • After I close my laptop, I'll write 3 things I'm grateful for

📊 Track Without Obsessing

Use a simple streak tracker on your phone. The goal isn't perfection — it's consistency. Missing one day is fine. Missing two days in a row? Reset and start again.

⚡ Lifestyle Fixes

Evidence-based tweaks for energy, sleep, and focus

😴 Sleep Architecture Reset

Poor sleep isn't just about quantity — it's about timing and quality. Here's what actually works:

  • Consistent wake time: More important than bedtime. Wake at 7 AM every day, even weekends
  • Sunlight exposure: Get 10 minutes of outdoor light within 30 minutes of waking
  • Caffeine cutoff: No caffeine after 2 PM (caffeine half-life is 5-6 hours)
  • Temperature drop: Keep bedroom at 18-19°C (65-67°F)

💡 Studies show: Consistent wake times improve sleep quality more than sleep duration alone

⚡ Energy Management (Not Time Management)

Your brain has ~4 hours of peak cognitive performance per day. Use them wisely:

  • Peak hours: First 2-4 hours after waking (for most people)
  • Protection strategy: No meetings, social media, or email during peak hours
  • Deep work blocks: 90-minute focused sessions with 15-minute breaks

🧠 Focus Nutrition

What you eat affects mental clarity within 30-60 minutes:

  • High-protein breakfast = sustained energy (eggs, Greek yogurt, nuts)
  • Avoid sugar crashes = skip refined carbs before important work
  • Hydration matters = even 2% dehydration reduces cognitive performance

🏃 Movement Micro-Habits

You don't need a gym membership. You need 10 minutes of movement every 2 hours:

  • Walk while taking calls
  • 5-minute stretch between study sessions
  • Take stairs instead of elevator

🎯 Study & Focus Systems

Learn smarter, not harder

🧠 Active Recall > Passive Reading

Reading notes repeatedly is one of the least effective study methods. Instead:

  • Close the book and write everything you remember
  • Quiz yourself before looking at answers
  • Teach concepts out loud as if explaining to someone else

💡 Research shows: Active recall improves retention by 150-200% vs re-reading

📆 Spaced Repetition Schedule

The forgetting curve is real. Review material at these intervals:

  • First review: 1 day after learning
  • Second review: 3 days later
  • Third review: 7 days later
  • Fourth review: 14 days later
  • Fifth review: 30 days later

🎯 The Pomodoro System (Modified)

Traditional Pomodoro (25 min work, 5 min break) is good, but this works better for deep study:

  • 50 minutes: Focused study (phone in another room)
  • 10 minutes: Active break (walk, stretch, hydrate)
  • After 3 sessions: Take a 30-minute break

📝 The Feynman Technique

Named after physicist Richard Feynman. Here's how it works:

  • Step 1: Write the concept at the top of a blank page
  • Step 2: Explain it in simple language (as if teaching a 12-year-old)
  • Step 3: Identify gaps in your understanding
  • Step 4: Review source material for those gaps
  • Step 5: Simplify your explanation even further

🔇 Environment Design

Your study environment affects focus more than willpower:

  • Remove ALL distractions before starting (not during)
  • Use website blockers (Freedom, Cold Turkey, Forest app)
  • Study in the same place consistently (location-based memory)
  • White noise or study music (avoid lyrical songs)
